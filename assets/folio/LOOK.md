# FOLIO — the look

What every FOLIO prompt is given as the look to follow: the storyboard director and the illustrator read
sections 4–7 below word for word. Change the look here and the next video follows it.

## 4. The picture

**Illustration.** Flat vector shapes, each filled with a soft two-stop gradient, a thin outline in a
darker shade of the fill (never black), one cel-shadow side. No texture, no grain, no paper noise on
the scenes. Detail is spent where the eye goes (faces, hands, the object of the sentence) and dropped
elsewhere.

**Light.** Every scene has one warm key light that you can see: a lamp with a bloom halo, a sun at
the vanishing point, a light shaft through a window. Dust motes float in it. Interiors are warm
(amber, brick, green wallpaper); exteriors are high-key (sky blue to near-white at the horizon).

**Depth.** Three to five planes. The foreground plane (beach grass, a lamp post, a railing) is
strongly blurred; the background of every close-up is blurred into hexagonal bokeh. Distant things
fade into the sky colour (atmospheric perspective).

**People.** Slim cartoon figures about 5.8 heads tall. Big anime-like eyes (dark iris, two white
highlights, a thick upper lash line), small nose line, small mouth, visible ear in 3/4 view.
Hair and moustaches are single shapes. Suits read by colour (Wilbur navy #3B4151, Orville brown
#665543) with lapel and vest lines; skin #F2CBAC. Characters keep exactly the same look in every
shot. They move like cut-out puppets: walking, raising an arm, turning the head, blinking — never
lip-synced, never talking to camera.

**Objects.** Drawn with care and slightly simplified: a toy helicopter, a bicycle, a glider with every
strut, an engine with four cylinders, a lunar module. Objects carry the story; when the narration
names one, the camera goes to it.

**Paper.** Diagrams live on a cream sheet (#E5DEC4, warmer #E2D4B9) with a faint two-level grid and
a soft vignette. Drawings on paper are sepia line art (#563B2A) with cream fills; motion and forces
are blue (#6EA2CA fill, #3F627C ink); the answer or the force that matters is orange (#EFC37B with a
glow); the thing that is wrong is struck through in red (#C13A29). Each sheet is numbered "Fig. 1",
"Fig. 2" bottom-left.

Numbers in serif are always old-style figures ("1878", "1901"). Words appear on their spoken word
(± 0.2 s) and leave with the shot. Nothing repeats the narration word for word except a quote.

## 6. Camera and motion

- Locked shots dominate. A move always has a reason: follow (the plane, the rider, the Flyer), push-in
  on a realisation (the cardboard box), reveal (pull back from the jumbo-jet comparison).
- Pushes run at 10–35 % scale per second only inside transitions; inside a shot they are slow (≤ 6 %
  over the shot).
- Everything alive moves a little: walk cycles, gestures, head turns, blinks, a spinning toy, grass,
  smoke, night streaks outside a train window, dust motes.
- Numbers count up with an ease-out; labels fade and rise a few pixels; lines draw on; cards pop in
  with a small overshoot; strikes draw left to right.

## 7. Transitions — the vocabulary

| Kind | How it looks | Length |
|---|---|---|
| Cloud whiteout | the camera tilts up into clouds until the frame is white, the next scene resolves out of it | 0.9 s |
| Light bloom | a warm glow blooms from the key light and dissolves to the next scene | 0.3–0.6 s |
| Zoom-through | the camera pushes into an object (a window, a box, the Moon) with blur; the inside is the next scene | 0.4–0.6 s |
| Whip pan | a horizontal pan with heavy motion blur | 0.5 s |
| Tilt | a fast vertical move to a new place or year (sky 1899 → beach 1900) | 0.3 s |
| Panel slide | the next scene slides over the old one like a wall panel, hard edge with a shadow | 0.3 s |
| Paper sheet | a diagram sheet slides up over a scene, or slides away to reveal one | 0.3 s |
| Object match | the object of one shot carries into the next (propeller, bicycle → glider) | — |
| Blob morph | a watercolour blob spreads on the paper and the next figure builds inside it | 0.5 s |
| Defocus dissolve | the picture goes soft while the next drawing draws on | 0.4 s |
| Cut | on an action (the toy leaves frame, the toy breaks) | — |
